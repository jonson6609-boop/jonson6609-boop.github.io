Starting an emergency fund when it feels impossible

Published August 10, 2026 · 5 min read · By Maya Reyes

The standard advice to save three to six months of expenses is correct, and also useless as a starting point, because it can sound so far away that people don't start at all. A smaller, more honest starting number gets money into a real account today, which matters more than the eventual size of the fund.

Start with $500, not three months of expenses

$500 covers a genuinely large share of common emergencies — a car repair, a broken appliance, an unexpected medical copay — without requiring months of saving before it's useful. Treat this as the actual first goal, not a checkpoint on the way to a bigger number.

Fund it with a fixed, boring amount

Pick a number you can hold every single month without strain — even $25 or $40. Set up an automatic transfer to a separate savings account on payday, before you see the money in your regular balance. At $40 a month, $500 takes about a year; at $80, about six months. Either pace beats waiting for a lump sum that may never arrive.

Keep it separate, but not locked away

A basic savings account, different from the one you spend from day to day, is enough — the goal is friction, not total inaccessibility. You want it hard enough to reach that you won't dip into it for a impulse purchase, but not so locked up that an actual emergency becomes its own crisis to access the money.

What counts as "using" the fund

Only genuine emergencies: something urgent, unexpected, and necessary. A sale on something you wanted isn't that, even if it feels time-sensitive. If you do use it, the next step is simply restarting the automatic transfer, not starting over from a blank slate of guilt.

$500 saved slowly beats three months of expenses saved never. The size of the eventual goal matters far less than whether the fund exists at all.

Set up the transfer this week

Pick a number you can hold every month, open a separate account if you don't have one, and set the transfer to happen automatically on payday. The first $500 is the milestone that actually matters — everything past that is just continuing a habit you've already proven you can keep.
