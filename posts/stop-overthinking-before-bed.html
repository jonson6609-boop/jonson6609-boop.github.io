How to stop overthinking before bed

Published July 24, 2026 · 5 min read · By Jonah Pierce

Trying to force a busy mind to be quiet usually backfires — the harder you push against a thought, the more space it seems to take up. The three techniques below don't try to silence the thinking. They give it somewhere else to go, so it stops circling.

1. Do a "brain dump" before you lie down

Keep a notebook or your phone by the bed, and for two minutes, write down everything on your mind — no order, no editing, just get it out of your head and onto the page. Most racing thoughts at bedtime are really your brain trying to make sure you don't forget something. Once it's written down, that job is done, and there's less for your mind to keep rehearsing.

2. Separate "tonight" thoughts from "tomorrow" thoughts

As you write, sort each item into one of two piles: something that needs attention tonight (rare), or something that can only be addressed tomorrow anyway (almost everything else). For the tomorrow pile, write one line: when you'll deal with it. That's often enough for your mind to let it go, since it's no longer at risk of being forgotten.

3. Give your attention somewhere neutral to land

Once the page is full, shift focus to something with no emotional weight — counting your breaths, naming five things you can hear, or slowly tensing and releasing each muscle group from your feet upward. This isn't about forcing calm; it's about giving your attention a low-effort task so it has somewhere to rest besides your to-do list.

The goal isn't to think less. It's to stop the same three or four thoughts from replaying on a loop, once they've each been acknowledged and given a place to go.

Why writing it down works better than trying to relax

"Just relax" is hard to act on, because it's not a specific instruction. Writing down what's circling, and giving each item a plan, is specific — it gives your mind a concrete signal that the thing has been handled, rather than asking it to simply stop caring about something it thinks still needs attention.

Try the brain dump tonight, even if it feels unnecessary. It usually takes under three minutes, and it's the step most people skip that ends up mattering most.

This is general information, not medical advice. If sleep difficulties or racing thoughts are frequent or distressing, it's worth talking to a doctor or therapist.
