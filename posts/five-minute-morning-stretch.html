A five-minute stretch routine for stiff mornings

Published July 27, 2026 · 5 min read · By Jonah Pierce

Waking up stiff isn't usually about age or a bad mattress — it's often just hours spent in one position with no movement at all. You don't need a full workout to undo it. Five short stretches, done slowly and in this order, loosen up the spots that tend to seize overnight.

1. Neck rolls (30 seconds)

While still sitting on the edge of the bed, slowly drop your chin to your chest, then roll your head gently to one shoulder, back, and to the other side. Keep it slow — this is about easing tightness, not stretching hard first thing.

2. Overhead reach (30 seconds)

Stand and reach both arms straight overhead, lacing your fingers together and turning your palms up. Lean gently to one side, then the other. This opens up the sides of your torso, which tend to stay compressed from sleeping curled up.

3. Standing forward fold (45 seconds)

Feet hip-width apart, slowly hinge forward from the hips, letting your arms and head hang loose. Bend your knees as much as you need to. This gently stretches the back of the legs and lower back, two spots that stiffen fastest overnight.

4. Cat-cow on the floor (45 seconds)

On hands and knees, alternate between arching your back up toward the ceiling and dipping it down, letting your belly drop. This is one of the fastest ways to loosen a stiff lower back, and most people feel a difference within a few rounds.

5. Seated spinal twist (45 seconds)

Sitting with legs extended or crossed, place one hand behind you and gently twist your torso toward it, holding for a few breaths, then switch sides. This wakes up the spine's rotation, which barely moves at all during sleep.

None of these need to be held long or pushed hard. The point is gentle movement through each area, not a deep stretch — that part can come later in the day if you want it.

Why order matters here

Starting at the neck and working down mirrors how stiffness tends to build overnight — small areas first, bigger muscle groups after. Jumping straight into a deep stretch on a cold, stiff body is more likely to feel uncomfortable than starting gently and building down the body.

Try it tomorrow morning before you reach for your phone. Five minutes, same five moves — it tends to matter more than the extra sleep you'd get by skipping it.

This is general information, not medical advice. If stiffness is severe, persistent, or painful, it's worth checking with a doctor or physical therapist.
