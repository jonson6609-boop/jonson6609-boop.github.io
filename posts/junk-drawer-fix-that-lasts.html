The junk drawer fix that actually lasts

Published August 16, 2026 · 4 min read · By Jonah Pierce

Emptying a junk drawer feels satisfying, and it lasts about three weeks. The problem isn't that the clean-out didn't work — it's that nothing changed about why it filled up in the first place, so it just refills the same way it always has.

The actual cause: no limit on what goes in

A junk drawer accepts anything, indefinitely, because there's no rule about what belongs there. Batteries, rubber bands, old receipts, a spare key, three dead pens — all of it gets a home simply because the drawer never says no. Fixing that requires an actual limit, not just a cleaner starting point.

The fix: pick five categories, and only five

Decide what genuinely belongs in the drawer — say, batteries, tape, a spare charger, scissors, and a notepad — and write the list somewhere you'll actually see it, even just a sticky note inside the drawer. Anything that isn't on that list doesn't go in, no exceptions, no "just this once."

What to do with the "just this once" items

They need a real home somewhere else, even a small one — a cup in the garage for spare keys, a specific spot for receipts you're keeping. The junk drawer refills fastest when other items don't have anywhere else to go, so it becomes the default for everything homeless.

Why five categories, specifically

Fewer than that and the drawer becomes too restrictive to be useful. More than that and it drifts back toward "anything goes," which is the exact problem you're fixing. Five is enough to be genuinely useful without becoming a catch-all again.

A junk drawer isn't a character flaw. It's a drawer with no rule about what belongs in it — give it one, and it mostly takes care of itself.

Reset yours this week

Empty it, pick your five categories, and only put those back. Everything else gets a real home elsewhere or goes in the bin. The list is what keeps it from becoming a junk drawer again — not how thoroughly you cleaned it out this time.
