What six months of decluttering my kitchen actually taught me

Published August 3, 2026 · 10 min read · By Jonah Pierce

I started decluttering my kitchen in February, and I finished, more or less, in late July. Six months for one room sounds absurd written down, and I want to explain why it actually took that long, because I think the length of time is the useful part of this story, not the method.

Why a whole weekend didn't work the first time

I tried the weekend-purge approach first, back in January. I pulled everything out of every cabinet on a Saturday, planning to sort it all by Sunday night. By Saturday evening I had every kitchen surface covered in piles, a headache, and a strong urge to just shove it all back where it came from — which is roughly what happened. The kitchen was, if anything, slightly messier than before I started, and I didn't touch it again for three weeks out of sheer avoidance.

That failure is what pushed me toward doing it slowly instead, which felt, at the time, like giving up on doing it properly. It wasn't.

Month one: the utensil drawer, and nothing else

In February I decided on one rule: fifteen minutes, one zone, whenever I had fifteen minutes to spare — no schedule, no deadline. The first zone was the utensil drawer, mostly because it was the one that annoyed me every single day. It took two sessions, about twenty-five minutes total, spread across four days because I kept getting interrupted.

I found three garlic presses. I have never once used more than one garlic press. That drawer alone told me something about how the whole kitchen had gotten this way — not through any single bad decision, but through years of buying a replacement for something I already owned but couldn't find in the moment.

Months two through four: the part that actually took time

The pantry was the slow one. Not because sorting it was hard, but because I kept finding expired items I felt genuinely bad throwing away — a bag of specialty flour I'd bought for one recipe two years earlier, three half-used bags of a spice blend I'd forgotten I owned duplicates of. I ended up doing the pantry in five separate short sessions across nearly two months, not because it needed that long physically, but because I kept stopping when the decision-making got uncomfortable and coming back later.

I mention the discomfort specifically because most decluttering advice treats it as a purely logistical task — sort, bin, done. For me, a meaningful chunk of it was quietly working through low-grade guilt about waste and money already spent, in small enough doses that it didn't turn into another Saturday I wanted to avoid entirely.

What I got wrong along the way

I over-bought storage containers in March, convinced that better bins would fix a problem that was actually about having too much stuff, not too little organization. I returned about half of them in April once it became obvious that an empty cabinet doesn't need a labeled bin — it just needs to stay empty.

Where it landed by July

By the time I got to the counters, the last zone, it took about ten minutes. Most of what had been cluttering them had already found a real home during the earlier zones, so there was very little left to actually decide on. That's the part I didn't expect going in: the last zone is disproportionately easy, because the earlier ones absorb most of the actual decision-making.

The slow version isn't a compromise on doing it properly. For me, it was the only version that survived contact with an actual, ordinary, busy life.

What I'd tell someone starting their own kitchen

Don't schedule a weekend for this if a weekend approach has failed you before — mine had, and going slower wasn't a lesser version of the same plan, it was the version that actually finished. Expect the middle zones to be the ones that drag, not the first or last. And it's fine, maybe even useful, to let a session end at "I decided some of it and I'll finish the rest later" rather than forcing a full zone in one sitting just because you started it.

Six months for one kitchen. It's done now, and unlike January, it's stayed that way — which, for me, was the actual point.
