The two-minute email rule that keeps your inbox sane

Published August 12, 2026 · 4 min read · By Maya Reyes

An overflowing inbox is rarely a volume problem — it's usually a few dozen emails that got opened, half-read, and left "for later" without ever getting a real decision made about them. Each one alone takes seconds to actually resolve. Left open, they just sit there, silently making the inbox feel heavier than it is.

The rule: if it takes under two minutes, finish it now

When you open an email, ask honestly: can I fully deal with this — reply, file, or delete — in under two minutes? If yes, do it immediately, right there, before moving to the next email. Don't mark it read and move on "to handle properly later."

What "later" actually means for most people

For quick emails, later rarely comes in any organized way. They just get reopened days afterward, re-read from scratch because the context has faded, and re-decided all over again — which takes longer in total than if it had been handled the first time. The two-minute rule exists specifically to catch that pattern before it starts.

What this rule isn't for

Anything that genuinely needs more than two minutes — a thoughtful reply, a decision that needs more information — shouldn't be rushed just to close it out. For those, move it to a specific "needs a real reply" folder or flag, so it's separated from the quick ones rather than mixed in with everything else.

Why this beats "inbox zero" as a goal

Inbox zero focuses on the count in the inbox, which can tempt people into archiving things without actually resolving them — technically zero, but nothing's really been handled. The two-minute rule focuses on actually finishing quick items, which naturally brings the count down as a side effect rather than the main goal.

The problem was never how many emails you have. It's how many of them are sitting half-decided.

Try it on your next inbox session

Open your inbox and apply the two-minute question to each new email as you go, starting today. You won't clear a backlog overnight, but going forward, far fewer quick emails will linger — and that's usually what makes an inbox feel manageable again.
