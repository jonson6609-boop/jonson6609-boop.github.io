Why buying in bulk sometimes costs more

Published August 15, 2026 · 4 min read · By Maya Reyes

Bulk packaging is designed to look like a deal, and often it is — but not always, and the difference isn't obvious just by comparing total prices. A $9 bulk pack can quietly cost more per unit than a $2 small one, and the only way to catch that is checking a number most shoppers skip.

The number that actually matters: unit price

Not the total price on the sticker — the price per ounce, per item, or per use, however the product is measured. Most grocery store shelves already print this in small text on the price tag, usually below the total price. It's easy to miss because it's small and unlabeled as "the important number," but it's the only fair way to compare a small pack to a bulk one.

Why the bigger pack isn't automatically cheaper per unit

Retailers price bulk items based on what will sell, not on a guaranteed discount curve. Promotional pricing on smaller packages, private-label brands sized differently than name brands, and simple pricing inconsistencies all mean the "value size" sometimes has a higher per-unit cost than the regular one sitting right next to it.

Where this trap shows up most

It's most common with snack foods, paper products, and anything with frequent promotional pricing on the smaller size. Staples you buy constantly — rice, pasta, cleaning supplies — are usually genuinely cheaper in bulk, but it's still worth the ten-second check rather than assuming.

The other real cost of bulk: waste

Even when the unit price is genuinely lower, bulk only saves money if you use all of it before it expires or you get tired of it. A large bag of something that goes stale before you finish it isn't a deal, regardless of what the unit price said — the math only works if the product actually gets used.

Bulk buying is a strategy, not a guarantee. The sticker price per unit is what tells you whether it's actually working this time.

Try it on your next shop

Before grabbing the bigger pack out of habit, glance at the unit price on both sizes. It takes about ten seconds and occasionally saves you from a "deal" that wasn't actually one.
