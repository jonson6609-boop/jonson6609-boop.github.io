Back up your phone photos before you need to

Published August 18, 2026 · 5 min read · By Maya Reyes

Photo via Unsplash

Nobody thinks about backups until a phone is lost, stolen, or dropped somewhere it shouldn't be. By then it's too late to do anything about the photos that were only ever in one place. None of the options below take long to set up, and you only need to pick one.

  1. Turn on automatic cloud backup (easiest, do this first) Both iPhones and Android phones have a built-in photo backup option in settings — iCloud Photos on iPhone, Google Photos backup on Android. Turn it on once, and every photo you take from then on backs up automatically over Wi-Fi, with nothing more to remember.
  2. Check that it's actually working Turning the setting on isn't the same as confirming it's running. Open the backup app and check for a status line like "Backup complete" or a recent sync date. If it shows an error or hasn't run in weeks, it's not protecting anything yet.
  3. Copy your most important photos to a physical drive too Cloud backups can fail, get hacked, or come with account issues you don't control. For the photos that would genuinely hurt to lose — not every photo, just the irreplaceable ones — copy them to an external drive or computer every few months as a second, independent copy.
  4. Set a recurring reminder to check both A phone reminder every three months, just to glance at your cloud backup status and confirm the physical copy is reasonably current. It takes two minutes and it's the step most people skip, right up until they need it.

Why two backups beat one

A single backup is still a single point of failure — if that one cloud account has an issue, everything tied to it is at risk together. A second, physical copy stored separately means one failure doesn't cost you everything, which is the entire point of backing up in the first place.

The five minutes it takes to turn on automatic backup is nothing compared to the years of photos it protects.

Do it today, not eventually

Check your phone's backup setting right now, before finishing this article. If it's already on, great — check that it's actually current. If it's off, turning it on takes under two minutes, and it's the kind of small task that only ever feels urgent after it's too late to matter.
