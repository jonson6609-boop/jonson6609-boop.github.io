The 24-hour rule that stops impulse purchases

Published August 6, 2026 · 4 min read · By Maya Reyes

Most impulse purchases don't survive a full day of thinking about them. The excitement that drives the "buy now" feeling is usually strongest in the first few minutes, and it fades faster than people expect — the 24-hour rule just gives that fading time to actually happen before your money leaves your account.

The rule

For any non-essential purchase over a set amount — pick a number that feels meaningful to you, $30 or $50 is common — wait 24 hours before buying. Add it to a cart or a notes list, close the tab, and revisit it the next day. If you still want it after a full day, buy it without guilt.

Why it works better than willpower alone

You're not telling yourself not to buy things. You're just moving the decision to a moment when you're not standing in front of the product, or scrolling past the ad, with the immediate pull of "want it now" driving the choice. A surprising number of items simply stop feeling necessary once that pull has faded.

What to do with the waiting time

Nothing special — just let a day pass. Some people find it helps to write down why they wanted the item in the moment; rereading that note the next day often makes the original reasoning look thinner than it felt at the time.

When to skip the rule

This isn't meant for genuine necessities — replacing a broken appliance, buying groceries, anything with real time pressure. It's specifically for the "this looks nice, I should get it" category of purchase, where a day of distance actually changes the decision.

You're not trying to stop spending. You're trying to make sure the spending you do is the kind you'd still choose tomorrow.

Try it on the next thing that catches your eye

The next time something makes you want to buy it on the spot, add it to a list instead and check back tomorrow. It costs nothing to wait a day, and it quietly filters out a good share of purchases you'd otherwise have regretted by the following week.
